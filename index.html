What is Tokenisation?

Tokenisation is the process of dividing a stream of text into smaller pieces, called tokens. These tokens can be words, subwords, characters or symbols, which depend on the design of the system. Tokenisation is a basic process of Natural Language Processing (NLP) as computers are not able to comprehend raw text as humans can. The machine learning model cannot analyse language before it's broken down into tokens.

For example, the sentence: "The cat is being chased by the dog." This sentence can be tokenised into individual word tokens: ["The", "cat", "is", "being", "chased", "by", "the", "dog"].

Simply put, tokenisation transforms continuous text into a structured form that can be manipulated by a computer. This process is applied in search engines, chatbots, translation systems and text analysis tools.

Large language models use tokenisation as a preprocessing stage before training or text generation.

How Tokenisation Works

The principle of tokenisation is based on the division of text into smaller units by using rules or algorithms. The algorithm used for tokenisation is based on the language and on the purpose of the task.

Step 1: Text Input

The input stage starts with the raw text. This may either be a sentence, paragraph or even a document.

The cats are chasing a mouse!
↓
Raw Text Input

Step 2: Preprocessing

After the text is inserted, the text would be normalised. This can include:

  • Changing all letters into lowercase
  • Removing punctuation
  • Eliminating extra spaces

For example: "Hello, World!" might become: "hello world"

"The cats are chasing a mouse!"
↓
the cats are chasing a mouse

Step 3: Splitting into Tokens

After normalising the text, the input is now ready. The system would divide the text into tokens. The output of the inputted text would be based on the tokenisation type chosen:

  • Word Tokenisation: splits the text by space

    • "I really like tokenisation" becomes ["I", "really", "like", "tokenisation"]

  • Character Tokenisation: splits text into individual characters

    • ["t", "o", "k", "e", "n", "i", "s", "a", "t", "i", "o", "n"]

the cats are chasing a mouse
↓
[ "the" "cats" "are" "chasing" "a" "mouse" ]

Step 4: Conversion into Numerical Form

Once the input is tokenised, every token is assigned a number. Computers cannot analyse words and therefore, the words need to be converted into numbers.

For example: "hello" → 321

Neural networks and language models take these numbers as their inputs.

"the" ↓ 104
"cats" ↓ 582
"are" ↓ 47
"chasing" ↓ 913
"a" ↓ 9
"mouse" ↓ 441

Types of Tokenisation

Word Tokenisation

Word Tokenisation is the process of breaking text down into the individual words.
It generally operates on the principle of dividing words into spaces and punctuations.

Sentence Tokenisation

Sentence Tokenisation splits a block of writing into individual sentences.
It generally marks the boundaries of a sentence with punctuations like full stops, question marks and exclamation marks.

Subword Tokenisation

Subword Tokenisation is the division of words into small parts called subwords.
The technique assists the models to learn about rare or unfamiliar words by breaking them into the smaller parts.

"unbelievableness" → subword split

  1. Byte Pair Encoding (BPE)

    2. Byte Pair Encoding is a subword tokenisation algorithm that compiles tokens by combining the most frequent pairs of characters or subword pairs in a dataset.

    BPE Merge Steps
  2. WordPiece

    3. Another popular subword tokenisation technique is WordPiece. It operates by dividing words into small units according to the frequency of occurrence in the training data.

    "playing" → WordPiece
    play ##ing
    The ## prefix marks a continuation; the subword attaches to what came before it.
  3. SentencePiece

    4. SentencePiece is a subword tokenisation algorithm which treats text as an unstructured stream of characters and does not use spaces to recognise word boundaries.

    "I like cats" → SentencePiece
    ▁I ▁like ▁cat s
    The ▁ marker represents a space in the raw stream; no pre-tokenisation needed.

Character Tokenisation

Character Tokenisation divides the text into characters rather than words or subwords.
Every letter, number or symbol becomes a token.
